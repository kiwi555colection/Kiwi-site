/ THE STORY

Every other bird flies.
Kiwis don't.

The world forgot them. And that's exactly where their strength lives.

I — THE ONES WHO STAYED

When the sky opens, when migration calls, when the whole world chases the horizon — every other bird spreads its wings and leaves. For higher. For farther. For bigger.

Kiwis lost their wings millions of years ago. Not from weakness — but because they never needed them. While the others chased the sky, kiwis mastered the ground. They learned to move in the dark, to hear what can't be heard, to survive while others vanished.

II — THE LONG NIGHT

On-chain, history repeats. Every day a new collection is born, promising to fly high — endless supply, a roadmap to the moon, utility without end. They flap hard, rise fast, then fall. One by one. The sky is full of wreckage that once promised everything.

Meanwhile, down on the dark forest floor, 555 kiwis walk quietly. They chase nothing. Promise no moon. They simply exist — small, patient, unshakeable.

The ones who fly highest fall hardest. The ones who stay grounded never fall at all.
